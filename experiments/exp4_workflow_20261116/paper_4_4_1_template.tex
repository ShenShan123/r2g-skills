\subsubsection{Dataset Validation: Reusable Workflows versus LLM-written Converters}
\label{sec:exp4-validation}
We compare two ways to construct datasets from completed physical designs. With their native harnesses,
DeepSeek and GPT independently develop and check reusable conversion scripts. With \R, the same models invoke
the installed generation and physical-validation tools and deliver a launch wrapper. Each submitted converter
or wrapper is frozen before evaluation and runs without further model calls or per-design repair.

\textbf{Setup.} Image and graph conversion are separate experiments, each with 18 development designs and
60 test designs: 6 and 20 per technology, respectively, across Nangate45, SKY130HD, and GF180 and different sizes.
Development and test design families are disjoint. Models receive the same output definitions and format checker,
with a development budget of 200 model requests and four hours. Each run uses eight CPU cores and 32\,GiB of memory.

\textbf{Evaluation.} Images contain five placement feature maps and routed congestion maps. Graphs contain all
five B--F views in homogeneous and heterogeneous form, evaluated on final-layout inputs. IR drop is outside this
experiment's input scope. An independent evaluator derives reference values from the physical artifacts;
a design passes only when every required topology, feature, and label check passes. We report complete designs
and resources in \cref{tab:conv-image,tab:conv-graph}. Cost includes model development and production, including
the workflow's validation; prior \R tool engineering, independent scoring, and queue time are excluded.
