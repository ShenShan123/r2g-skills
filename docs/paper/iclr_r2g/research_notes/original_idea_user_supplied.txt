1. Introduction
Modern EDA workflows are long, tool-intensive, and difficult to operate reliably. A practical RTL-to-layout flow usually requires synthesis, floorplanning, placement, clock-tree synthesis, routing, extraction, DRC/LVS checking, timing analysis, and repeated diagnosis across heterogeneous tools. This high operational complexity raises the barrier for non-expert users and makes large-scale data generation difficult to reproduce.
At the same time, machine learning for physical design depends on circuit corpora that are large, diverse, reproducible, and tied to meaningful physical labels. Recent work has already advanced several parts of this problem: DeepCircuitX, ForgeEDA, OpenRTLSet, and CircuitNet 3.0 expand open-source RTL corpora; EDALearn, ChiPBench, and HighTide provide reproducible RTL-to-signoff or RTL-to-GDS infrastructure; and Verilog-to-PyG, CircuitOps, AiEDA, EDA-Schema-V2, and R2G translate design artifacts into graph, vector, or multimodal learning representations. These results rule out a broad novelty claim based only on collecting RTL, running an EDA flow, or serializing graphs. The remaining research question is whether these capabilities can be integrated into a continual, failure-aware production loop that binds each graph sample to one qualified physical-design run and improves its own recovery policy through controlled evidence.
We present Agentic RTL-to-Graph, an agentic system for constructing physical-design graph datasets from open-source RTL. The system is not positioned as the first RTL corpus, RTL-to-GDS flow, or circuit-graph converter. Instead, it operationalizes their difficult intersection: acquiring previously unseen RTL candidates, qualifying them for synthesis, promoting viable designs into ORFS-based physical implementation, diagnosing and recovering from flow failures, applying explicit signoff and provenance admission rules, and publishing training-ready multi-view graphs. The intended outcome is a campaign-scale data factory in which successful, rejected, and recovered runs all produce structured evidence rather than a collection assembled only from surviving designs.
The central hypothesis is therefore narrower and testable: an evidence-gated agent can improve the yield and auditability of continual open-RTL-to-graph construction beyond a fixed scripted pipeline, without admitting graphs whose physical or provenance status is incomplete. This hypothesis requires evaluation of autonomy, recovery, admission precision, cross-design transfer, and production cost, rather than relying only on the number of generated designs.

2. Related Work
2.1 LLM Agents for RTL-to-GDS and ASIC Flow Automation
Recent work has established several forms of agentic EDA automation. ChatEDA, IICPilot, MCP4EDA, AutoEDA, ASIC-Agent, NL2GDS, and Design Conductor demonstrate tool orchestration ranging from script generation to autonomous specification-to-GDS execution. OpenROAD Agent adds execution-guided script correction, while HighTide uses agent skills and per-design decision logs to maintain an evolving RTL-to-GDS benchmark suite. These systems make a generic claim of "LLM-controlled RTL-to-GDS" insufficient. The relevant distinction for the present work is its data-production objective and the coupling of acquisition, graph admission, and evidence-gated repair learning across many independent RTL subjects.

2.2 Physical Design Datasets for Machine Learning
Dataset and representation work now covers nearly every individual transformation required by an RTL-to-graph factory. OpenABC-D established large-scale synthesis trajectories; CircuitNet, EDALearn, ChiPBench, EDA-schema, and EDA-Schema-V2 provide stage-resolved physical-design data; Verilog-to-PyG and CircuitOps provide graph-oriented conversion infrastructure; DeepCircuitX, ForgeEDA, OpenRTLSet, and CircuitNet 3.0 substantially expand open RTL sources; AiEDA formalizes Design-to-Vector across graph, net, path, and patch granularities; and R2G standardizes five stage-aware circuit-graph views. The present work must therefore be evaluated on the integration and governance of continual production, not on any one of these already established components.

2.3 Self-improving and Tool-feedback EDA Agents
A third line of work studies how EDA agents improve through execution feedback, memory, or reusable skills. OpenROAD Agent corrects generated scripts using tool errors; Dr. RTL distills reusable optimization skills from parallel tool-grounded trials; VeriAgent maintains evolving memory for PPA-aware RTL generation; Trace2Skill extracts skills from execution traces; and RTL-BenchMT applies agents to dynamic benchmark maintenance. HighTide also records per-design tuning rationale as long-term memory. These mechanisms are close to run-history diagnosis and Recipe reuse, but they optimize designs, scripts, tools, or benchmark cases rather than governing the admission and continual construction of physical-design graph samples.

3. Method
The method is organized around the system architecture, continual RTL acquisition, signoff-aware physical implementation, evidence-gated learning, and qualified multi-view graph construction. The system combines an adaptive Agent with deterministic EDA stages and machine-readable transition gates. This separation allows campaign-scale autonomy while preserving reproducibility and preventing the Agent from bypassing synthesis, signoff, provenance, or dataset-admission requirements.
3.1 System Architecture and Agentic Decision Loop
Agentic RTL-to-Graph is a continual data-production system that transforms open-source RTL into signoff-aware, multi-view graph datasets. Before a campaign starts, the system detects host capabilities, resolves or provisions the required EDA tools and technology assets, pins the resulting environment, and verifies readiness. This deterministic prerequisite ensures that downstream stages share consistent tool, platform, and graph-generation dependencies; the experimental configuration is reported in Section 4.1.
toolchain readiness
-> open-source RTL acquisition and qualification
-> physical implementation and signoff
-> multi-view graph construction
-> dataset admission and manifest publication
-> run-history update and continued acquisition
The architecture separates adaptive control from deterministic transformation. Repository processing, synthesis, physical implementation, signoff extraction, graph construction, and database updates are executed by deterministic programs. The Agent observes their structured outputs, selects the next admissible action, coordinates recovery, and escalates unresolved cases. Persistent state allows interrupted runs to resume and failed candidates to be retried without repeating completed work.
The observation space includes candidate provenance, configuration readiness, stage completion, timing and routing summaries, DRC/LVS results, graph-quality reports, and run-history evidence. The action space includes acquiring or retrying a candidate, promoting a synthesis-qualified design, resuming an affected stage, applying an admissible Recipe, launching a controlled A/B trial, generating graphs, or escalating an unresolved case.
observe structured artifacts
-> classify the outcome or failure
-> retrieve and rank admissible Recipes
-> apply one bounded intervention
-> rerun the affected stage
-> evaluate signoff and dataset quality
-> ingest the resulting evidence
-> promote, demote, continue, or escalate
Synthesis qualification controls entry into physical implementation, signoff and provenance evidence control entry into the formal graph corpus, and Recipe lifecycle state controls adaptive repair. These deterministic gates cannot be overridden by an Agent decision or an auxiliary score. Unknown symptoms and repeated non-convergence are recorded as escalations, allowing the campaign to continue with other designs rather than blocking globally.
3.2 Autonomous RTL Acquisition and Candidate Qualification
The acquisition stage continually expands the design pool beyond a fixed benchmark suite. It discovers public RTL repositories, materializes selected revisions, identifies candidate top modules and source bundles, and records repository URL, revision, source paths, language, license and filtering decisions. Incremental scan state prevents unchanged repositories from being repeatedly treated as new candidates.
Each candidate is normalized into an isolated synthesis project and evaluated with a synthesis-only flow. The frontend resolves include files and parameters, attempts supported language conversion when necessary, and maps the design to the target cell library. Candidates are deduplicated at both the RTL and mapped-netlist levels so that renamed or reorganized copies do not inflate the corpus. A preliminary netlist graph and structural statistics provide an additional integrity check before physical implementation.
Candidate outcomes are stored as explicit states rather than a binary repository-level decision. A qualified candidate has a valid mapped netlist, unique signatures, graph output, and complete provenance. Synthesis failures, conversion failures, duplicates, and excluded sources retain their reasons and remain available for analysis or policy-controlled retry. Quality scoring and publication eligibility prioritize useful and sufficiently complex designs while preserving an auditable record of rejected candidates.
3.3 Signoff-aware Physical Implementation
Synthesis-qualified candidates are promoted into self-contained physical-design projects. Promotion carries forward the validated RTL bundle, top module, frontend settings, target platform, and clock assumptions, then constructs the backend configuration and timing constraints. A readiness gate rejects incomplete or inconsistent projects before expensive physical implementation begins.
The backend executes synthesis, floorplanning, placement, clock-tree synthesis, detailed routing, parasitic extraction, timing analysis, DRC, and LVS. Stage outcomes are represented by structured status records, allowing the campaign to resume from the affected stage and to distinguish design failures from incomplete or interrupted runs. Repair is bounded: each intervention is evaluated against the previous violation state, and repeated non-improvement terminates the local loop instead of consuming unbounded compute.
The physical run produces a provenance-bound evidence package containing stage completion, configuration lineage, timing and routing summaries, parasitic availability, DRC/LVS status, antenna status, caveats, and failure classification. These records are consumed by both the learning loop and the graph-admission gate.
3.4 Run-history Learning and A/B-gated Recipe Lifecycle
After every complete, failed, or partial run, the system ingests the outcome, failure signature, violation snapshot, applied intervention, configuration lineage, and resulting quality metrics into a run-history store. For a known symptom, the Agent retrieves repair Recipes and ranks them using relevant evidence for the same check type, platform, symptom class, and design context. Raw logs and isolated successful runs are retained as evidence but are not treated as trusted policy by themselves.
Recipe adoption follows an explicit lifecycle. New or modified Recipes remain non-live and are compared with a control configuration on matched subjects. The treatment arm must be provenance-complete, differ from the control only through the intended intervention, produce judgeable results, satisfy required usability and signoff criteria, and improve the target-aware outcome. Auxiliary outcome scores cannot compensate for an unusable result or a new regression.
Verifiable net-positive evidence promotes a Recipe into live ranking; net-negative or regressive evidence moves it to shadow or demoted status; inconclusive evidence leaves it non-live. Live performance is monitored so that a previously promoted Recipe can be withdrawn after repeated regression. Cases outside the known symptom catalog are escalated for analysis and may yield new shadow Recipes for later controlled validation.
3.5 Multi-view Graph Dataset Construction and Quality Control
Graph construction begins only after the physical artifacts and their run provenance have been identified. The system extracts structural features from the mapped netlist and physical database and derives supervision signals from placement, routing, timing, parasitic, and signoff results. Missing or stale feature and label products are regenerated against the selected final layout before graph assembly.
Dataset admission is controlled by a signoff gate. A formal sample requires a completed backend run, an acceptable DRC result, an acceptable or explicitly skipped LVS result, and no known residual routing violations. Missing, failed, stale, or provenance-inconsistent evidence blocks formal dataset generation. BEOL-only DRC, skipped LVS, incomplete antenna coverage, and timing violations are retained as explicit caveats rather than silently interpreted as full signoff. This policy separates dataset usability from the mere existence of a final DEF file.
The output contains five related physical graph views. View b preserves gate, net, I/O-pin, and instance-pin nodes. View c folds instance pins, view d folds nets, view e retains pin-level entities after folding gates and nets, and view f represents net connectivity primarily between gates and I/O pins. These views expose different trade-offs between structural fidelity, graph size, and message-passing distance while sharing aligned design provenance and labels. A synthesis-netlist graph is also retained when available.
Every generated sample is accompanied by a manifest that records design and platform identity, graph-view statistics, feature and label schemas, missing-label rates, RC coverage, signoff status, caveats, and artifact provenance. Downstream users can therefore select strict signoff-clean samples, include qualified samples with explicit caveats, or filter by label completeness without inferring quality from filenames alone.

4.1 Toolchain Provisioning, Platforms, and Reproducibility
Each campaign begins from a declared host environment and executes a deterministic readiness procedure that detects available capabilities, resolves missing dependencies, provisions the required tools and technology assets when permitted, pins the selected paths and versions, and verifies the resulting environment before any design enters the flow. The evaluated stack includes Yosys for synthesis, OpenROAD-flow-scripts and OpenROAD for physical implementation, KLayout, Netgen, and Magic for physical verification, OpenRCX for parasitic extraction, and PyTorch Geometric for graph serialization.
The experimental record reports tool and platform versions, technology libraries and verification decks, host hardware, resource limits, random seeds, timeout and retry budgets, graph-environment dependencies, and the LLM configuration used by the Agent. Detailed installation commands and directory layouts remain in the artifact documentation; the paper reports the information required to reproduce the evaluated environment and interpret runtime or failure results.
4.2 Evaluation Questions and Metrics
The evaluation is organized around six research questions that test dataset production, reliability, data quality, diversity, downstream utility, and learning behavior. Continual-production claims are evaluated through a sustained campaign rather than isolated demonstration runs.

4.3 Baselines and Ablation Design
Dataset-side comparisons prioritize the closest functional neighbors rather than only similarly named systems. ForgeEDA tests overlap in Internet RTL collection and PyG dataset generation; AiEDA and CircuitOps test overlap in vectorization and graph-oriented data infrastructure; R2G tests multi-view graph construction; CircuitNet 3.0, EDA-Schema-V2, EDALearn, and ChiPBench test corpus scale, stage coverage, and reproducible physical-flow data; and HighTide tests evolving RTL-to-GDS curation and incremental execution. Flow-agent comparisons include reproducible systems such as AutoEDA and OpenROAD Agent, while Design Conductor provides a reference for long-horizon autonomous chip implementation. PostEDA-Bench remains a complementary reference for signoff diagnosis and repair rather than an end-to-end dataset-construction baseline. Comparisons should use compatible released artifacts and explicitly mark dimensions that cannot be reproduced from public code or data.
Ablations isolate the contribution of continual acquisition, resumable stage control, structured diagnosis, run-history retrieval, A/B-gated Recipe promotion, and signoff-aware dataset admission. The evaluation compares the full system against variants without adaptive diagnosis, without learned Recipe retrieval, without A/B lifecycle control, without the signoff gate, and with a fixed design pool. Case studies report both successful recovery and non-convergent or rejected designs so that improvements are not inferred only from surviving samples.
5. Conclusions and Limitations
The expected conclusion is that agentic automation can be used not only to finish individual EDA tasks, but also to construct reusable training data for EDA-ML. By combining RTL acquisition, validation, physical implementation, signoff health tracking, and graph conversion, the system can provide a scalable path toward more diverse graph datasets.
The main limitations should also be stated clearly. First, open-source RTL quality is uneven, and many repositories may fail due to missing dependencies, unsupported constructs, unclear top modules, or synthesis issues. Second, full physical implementation is computationally expensive and can fail due to routing, antenna, DRC, LVS, or tool-environment problems. Third, signoff coverage may vary by platform; for example, a design may pass BEOL-only DRC while full DRC or LVS remains unavailable or too expensive. Finally, the graph dataset quality depends on accurate feature extraction, label completeness, and manifest integrity, so downstream users must consume the quality metadata rather than treating every graph file as equally reliable.

The introduction should begin from the practical difficulty of EDA flows and the scarcity of reusable EDA-ML datasets. The related-work section should avoid claiming that EDA flow automation is new. Instead, it should explain that prior agents mainly automate or optimize design flows, while prior datasets mainly rely on fixed or curated design sources. The proposed system is positioned as a bridge between these two directions.
The experimental section should identify at least one dataset-side baseline and one flow-agent-side baseline if reproducibility permits. EDA-Schema-V2 and CircuitNet are strong dataset-side comparisons. AutoEDA is a useful open-source flow-agent comparison. MCP4EDA is highly relevant conceptually, but code accessibility should be verified before using it as an executable baseline.
