011                                                 A BSTRACT
012
013            Constructing learning datasets from public RTL requires reliable source quali-
014            ﬁcation, physical-design recovery, and preservation of circuit semantics across
015            implementation stages. We present Agentic RTL-to-Graph, an evidence-gated
016            workﬂow that connects these tasks through provenance-bound artifacts, frozen
017            repair knowledge, and stage-aware graph construction. Repair proposals are val-
018
               idated on disjoint development designs and promoted into reusable recipes. The
               graph builder preserves canonical synthesis identities across four prediction stages
019
               and separates stage-permitted inputs from post-route supervision. Four comple-
020
               mentary studies evaluate acquisition, repair transfer, graph conversion, and down-
021            stream utility. R2G qualiﬁes all 200 requested RTL designs from 122 repositories
022            in 33.52 hours, compared with 119 designs for the strongest of seven tool-enabled
023            LLM baselines. On 13 held-out repair challenges, candidate reuse repairs eight
024            designs; promoted recipes repair all seven covered DRC cases with seven ﬂow tri-
025            als and no deployment-time LLM calls. In a 24-design conversion benchmark, the
026            strongest LLM-developed converter attains perfect audited identity and topology
027            F1, yet only 25.12% numerical feature and 13.33% label correct coverage; R2G
028            passes all seven semantic groups on every design. Finally, downstream models
029
               on 278 designs show that post-placement data reduces wirelength and effective-
               resistance MAE by 61.7% and 35.8% relative to post-synthesis inputs. Removing
030
               placement geometry increases their errors by 170.2% and 55.0%, respectively.
031
               These results establish graph conversion as an identity- and physics-preserving
032            data construction task, alongside qualiﬁed acquisition and reusable repair.

035   1    I NTRODUCTION
036
037   Modern electronic design automation (EDA) ﬂows coordinate synthesis, ﬂoorplanning, placement,
038   clock-tree synthesis, routing, extraction, and physical veriﬁcation. Building learning datasets from
039   public RTL adds a second layer of work: recovering compilation closure, resolving implementation
040   failures, aligning circuit entities across stages, and extracting physical labels with explicit prediction-
041   time availability. Dataset production therefore depends on a sequence of decisions about which
042   sources to accept, how to recover failed runs, and when the resulting graphs satisfy the intended
043   learning contract.
044   OpenROAD provides physical implementation infrastructure (The OpenROAD Project, n.d.);
045   ForgeEDA and HighTide support circuit collection and evolving RTL-to-GDS benchmarks (Shi
046   et al., 2025; Goldblatt et al., 2026); AiEDA and CircuitOps connect physical-design artifacts to
047   programmatic data access (Qiu et al., 2025; Liang et al., 2023). Building on these capabilities, we
048   address the evidence handoffs between acquisition, physical recovery, and graph admission. Each
049   handoff has a distinct acceptance condition: reproducible synthesis for a source candidate, validated
050
      outcomes for a reusable repair, and consistent identities, topology, and labels for a graph sample.
051   We present Agentic RTL-to-Graph, implemented by the R2G skills workﬂow. Structured run ob-
052   servations guide candidate selection, scoped repair, stage resumption, and admission; deterministic
053   programs execute these actions and preserve their provenance. A candidate-to-recipe lifecycle turns
      development-time repair proposals into policies that can be reused without further LLM calls. A

      four-stage graph contract anchors entities to a canonical synthesis netlist and separates permitted
055   input features from post-route supervision. Independent semantic checks make these requirements
056   measurable at the output boundary. This matters for learning because a correct connectivity graph
057   can still lack the physical features and target values needed to deﬁne a supervised sample. R2G treats
058   entity identity, physical quantities, stage visibility, and validity masks as one conversion contract.
059
      Our contributions are fourfold. First, we organize RTL-to-graph construction around explicit source,
060
      run, policy, and graph evidence contracts. Second, we implement and evaluate frozen repair reuse
061   with disjoint proposal, validation, and held-out designs. Third, we construct four-stage graphs with
062   independent semantic acceptance checks that cover identity, topology, numerical features, labels,
063   masks, and causal stage restrictions. Fourth, we show on a ﬁxed 278-design split that the admitted
064   graphs retain useful stage-speciﬁc physical signal: placement geometry sharply improves wirelength
065   and effective-resistance prediction, while graph topology helps selected targets rather than univer-
066   sally dominating feature-only models. Complementary experiments also demonstrate 200 qualiﬁed
067   RTL designs from 122 repositories, eight repairs on 13 held-out challenges using the candidate
068   bank, and fully accepted graph outputs for all 24 test conversion designs. Throughout the paper,

074

089   checkable outcomes (Liu et al., 2026). Our repair experiment examines a complementary setting:

091   ﬁxed action policy. Separating these phases measures the transfer of executable repair knowledge
092   and its deployment cost.

098   R2G comprises four cooperating components: environment provisioning, RTL acquisition, phys-
099   ical implementation and repair, and graph construction. Provisioning resolves tool and platform
100   dependencies and records their conﬁguration. Acquisition handles repository discovery, immutable
101   revisions, synthesis closure, deduplication, and release eligibility. The physical-design component
102   executes ORFS and records implementation and signoff outcomes. The graph component converts
103   these artifacts into stage-aware datasets. Figure 1 summarizes the evidence handoffs.
104   A source record binds repository, commit, top module, compilation inputs, source hashes, and li-
105   cense evidence. A physical run binds conﬁguration, toolchain, stage artifacts, and checking reports.
106   A graph record binds its input artifacts, canonical entity mapping, feature cutoff, label sources, and
107   validation results. These bindings support traceable acceptance decisions and distinguish method
      failures, unavailable dependencies, and incomplete runs.

      2.2    A DAPTIVE C ONTROL AND RUN H ISTORY
135
136   The operating workﬂow observes structured candidate records, stage status, failure signatures, phys-
137   ical checks, and graph reports. Admissible decisions include selecting or retrying a candidate, pro-
138   moting a synthesis-qualiﬁed project into physical implementation, applying a scoped repair, re-
139   suming an affected stage, scheduling a controlled trial, or escalating a nonconvergent case. Deter-
140   ministic programs execute these actions and record outcomes. “Agentic” describes this observation–
141   decision–execution organization. RTL acquisition and held-out recipe reuse operate without external
142   LLM calls.
143   The engineer-loop implementation maintains an append-only ledger with pending, ﬂow, signoff,
144   ﬁxing, clean, escalated, and abandoned states. Stage reuse is tied to recorded artifact lineage. Run
145   histories retain failures as well as successes, so recovery does not erase the original outcome. The
146   operational recipe machinery supports candidate, promoted, and shadow states, including evidence-
147   based withdrawal from learned retrieval. Static bootstrap strategies and learned indexed recipes have
148   distinct eligibility paths. The repair experiment evaluates the frozen promotion-and-reuse protocol
149   below.

184
185   The repair study separates proposal, validation, and deployment. On the proposal split, LLMs sug-
186
      gest structured conﬁguration actions. Schema and action-policy checks reject invalid parameters,

187
      evidence, retain complete provenance, and avoid clean-sentinel regressions. Each executable pay-
188
      load is hashed; exploration outcomes do not themselves count as promotion votes.
189

191   cycle evidence. Let W and L be independent-family wins and losses, Wv validation wins, and R
192

193                        promote(c) = [W ≥ 2] ∧ [W > L] ∧ [Wv ≥ 1] ∧ [¬R].                             (3)

      4                                        D ISCUSSION
469
470   The studies establish complementary acceptance boundaries. Acquisition supplies qualiﬁed sources;
471   frozen repair reuse converts development evidence into executable actions. The converter baseline
472   shows that perfect audited topology can still omit physical values and supervision. R2G connects
473   these layers through canonical identity, unit-aware extraction, stage visibility, and explicit validity.
474
      Downstream results close the evidence chain: geometry materially improves wirelength and resis-
      tance prediction, whereas hold slack is insensitive and feature-only models outperform GINE on
475
      several targets. Rather than claiming universal model dominance, the data support controlled tests
476
      of when topology and geometry matter.
477
478   Reproducibility. The ﬁxed split, three-seed checkpoints, predictions, and per-design metrics are
479   archived; a local rescore reproduces all 142,996 targets in a representative test run within cross-
480
      device tolerance.
481
482   Conclusion. R2G connects qualiﬁed acquisition, frozen repair knowledge, and physically
483   grounded graph construction through evidence contracts. It supplies 200 qualiﬁed sources, repairs
484   eight of 13 held-out challenges, passes all core audits on 24 conversion designs, and produces stage-
485   aware graphs with measurable physical signal: an auditable path from public RTL to aligned topol-
      ogy, geometry, and supervision.
